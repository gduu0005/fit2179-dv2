Connected Australia?
Mapping the Digital Divide

Australia has one of the world's highest internet penetration rates — yet for millions of Australians, the digital world remains out of reach or unaffordable. This visualisation explores who is connected, who is not, and what it costs.

Section 01 — Geography

Where You Live Shapes Your Digital Life

Digital inclusion in Australia is not uniformly distributed. The Australian Capital Territory leads the nation with a score of 79.5, while the Northern Territory — home to many remote and First Nations communities — scores just 62.3. The gap between the best and worst performing states is over 17 points.

Section 02 — Progress Over Time

Progress is Real — But Uneven

Australia's national digital inclusion score has climbed steadily from 63.5 in 2021 to 73.6 in 2025. Internet access among older Australians has grown significantly — those aged 75+ went from 30% access in 2017 to 65% in 2024. But the gap between groups has not closed at the same pace.

Section 03 — Who Is Left Behind

One in Five Australians Is Digitally Excluded

Digital exclusion is not random. It concentrates among specific groups: older Australians, those with lower education, First Nations people, people with disability, and low-income households. For these groups, being offline is not a choice — it is the result of structural barriers.

First Nations Digital Gap by Remoteness

ADII score comparison between First Nations and other Australians across remoteness categories, 2025.

40.9% of First Nations Australians are digitally excluded — twice the national rate. In very remote communities, the gap widens to 22.8 points, driven by limited infrastructure, higher data costs relative to income, and fewer digital literacy pathways.

Section 04 — The Cost Problem

Connectivity Comes at a Price Many Can't Afford

The national Affordability score is 70.3 — the lowest of the three ADII dimensions. Almost one in five Australians (19.4%) compromise on internet speed or data allowance to manage costs. For unemployed households, that rises to 38.2%. Price is a genuine barrier, not an inconvenience.
